CrossDrop Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2, 2026 · Publisher: GranTurismoGroup

The short version: CrossDrop collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no ads, and no third-party code that talks to the internet. Your files travel directly between your own devices over your local network and never pass through us or anyone else.

Who this covers

This policy applies to the CrossDrop apps for Windows, macOS, Android and iOS. CrossDrop is a tool for sending files between nearby devices that are on the same local network (Wi‑Fi or Ethernet).

What CrossDrop does on your device

  • Finding nearby devices. To let your devices see each other, CrossDrop announces itself on your local network (mDNS / Bonjour) and, on phones, over Bluetooth Low Energy. The announcement contains a random identifier that changes every time the app starts, plus a display name. The display name defaults to your device’s name (on a PC, its computer name) and you can change it in Settings. These announcements stay on your local network; they are not sent to the internet.
  • Sending files. Files you choose are sent straight to the device you pick over a direct local connection. They are encrypted end to end (X25519 key exchange with ChaCha20‑Poly1305), and both devices show the same 6‑digit code so you can confirm you are talking to the right device.
  • Send to anyone (QR code). You can share files with a device that does not have CrossDrop. The app starts a temporary web page on your local network, protected by a long random link that is shown as a QR code. Anyone on your network who has that link can download the files until you stop sharing. This page uses plain HTTP on your local network (not HTTPS), so share it only on networks you trust.
  • Wireless drive. If you turn on the drive feature, the folder you choose is made available to devices on your local network, read-only unless you allow editing, optionally behind a 4‑digit passcode. It stops when you press “Stop sharing” or quit the app.
  • Receiving files. Incoming files wait in CrossDrop’s private Inbox until you open, move or delete them. You accept or decline every transfer.

What is stored, and where

Everything CrossDrop keeps is stored only on your device:

  • files you have received (the Inbox);
  • a local list of past transfers (device name, file names, sizes, time, and the outcome);
  • your display name and app settings.

On Windows this lives in the app’s local data folder for your user account. You can delete received files and the transfer list from inside the app (Inbox and Transfers). Uninstalling the Microsoft Store version on Windows, or the app on Android or iOS, also removes its data.

What CrossDrop does not do

  • It does not collect, sell or share personal data — nothing leaves your local network.
  • It has no accounts, sign-in, email address or phone number requirement.
  • It contains no analytics, crash-reporting, tracking or advertising code.
  • It does not connect to the internet for any of its features.

Children

CrossDrop does not collect any personal information from anyone, including children, and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy
